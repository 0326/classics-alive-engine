# Historical integrity

Separate four layers in every review:

- **Textual fact**: directly present in the selected edition.
- **Interpretation**: a reasoned reading of the text or a documented scholarly position.
- **Dramatization**: connective tissue needed for scene, pacing, or player agency.
- **Counterfactual**: an explicitly marked branch that departs from the canonical account.

When sources conflict, record the conflict and the chosen editorial policy in the adaptation brief. Do not merge incompatible dates, quotations, or motivations into one unmarked statement. Do not fabricate dialogue in quotation marks. For invented dialogue, use a narrator or UI label that makes its status clear when the distinction matters.

Historical confidence is not the same as moral approval. Avoid turning a canonical outcome into a claim that the character “should” have acted differently unless the text or the adaptation brief makes that interpretation explicit.
